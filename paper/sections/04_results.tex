\section{Experimentation and Results}
\label{sec:results}

Six pre-registered hypotheses (H1--H6, H1 comprising two tests, H5 covering the whole of RQ4) are evaluated against the criteria fixed before any result was computed; a supplementary, post-hoc probe extends H2's design within the dynamic benchmark itself (Section~\ref{sec:results-h2supp}). Table~\ref{tab:hypotheses} summarises every prediction and verdict; the remainder of this section reports each in turn, in the order the paper's discussion follows rather than the order of the research questions, since H2's causal result frames how the more equivocal observational results should be read.

\subsection{H2: Fine-Tuning Inflates the Static Benchmark, Not the Dynamic One}
\label{sec:results-h2}
Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct, LoRA-fine-tuned directly on ULT's own repair-verified ground truth (\HTwoSeenN{} problems), gains \HTwoSeenGain{} percentage points (95\% CI \HTwoSeenGainCI{}) on that same seen-ULT split, with \HTwoPreMutkTotal{} of \HTwoPreMutkTotal{} post-fine-tune completions byte-identical to their training target -- direct evidence of memorisation, not transfer. A control condition measures how much the model improved at test writing in general: the dynamic-benchmark tiers L1--L3, which the fine-tuning run never trained on, move by only \HTwoLOneLThreeGain{} points (CI \HTwoLOneLThreeGainCI{}). The complexity-matched dynamic control (L4) moves by exactly \HTwoLFourGain{} points. PLT, ULT's leaked twin, was evaluated on the same checkpoint. After subtracting the L1--L3 control, it gains \HTwoPltDid{} points (CI \HTwoPltDidCI{}) -- smaller than the seen-ULT gain but clearly non-zero. The fine-tuning run never saw PLT directly, so this indicates that leaked content confers a partial, indirect memorisation benefit. % cite:external -- "1.5" is the model's own parameter-count identifier (Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct), not a result value

\subsection{H2-Supplementary: Contamination Within the Dynamic Benchmark}
\label{sec:results-h2supp}
Post-hoc, not one of the six pre-registered hypotheses: the canonical experiment above leaks a static benchmark and shows the dynamic benchmark does not inflate; this probe tests the reverse. If a single dynamic-benchmark instantiation is itself leaked into training, does inflation follow the leaked artefact rather than the benchmark as a whole? Two Qwen models (1.5B, 7B) are each fine-tuned three ways on instantiation 1: on their own prior generations restricted to that instantiation (self-contamination), on a stronger model's -- GPT-5's -- generations on the same instantiation (cross-model contamination), and, as a non-leaked comparison point, on instantiation 1's reference outputs filtered only by whether they execute rather than whether they are correct. Both leaked conditions show a seen-versus-fresh gap in the expected direction at both model sizes; the non-leaked condition shows none, within noise. The decline appears in both cross-model conditions and in neither self-contamination condition: cross-model contamination degrades general capability as well as inducing memorisation, while self-contamination induces memorisation without the same capability cost. Held-out execution rate -- whether the generated suite runs at all, independent of correctness -- declines by epoch 30 for both cross-model-trained checkpoints (1.5B: \HSuppOneFiveBDeclineEpochZero{}\% to \HSuppOneFiveBDeclineEpochThirty{}\%; 7B: \HSuppSevenBDeclineEpochZero{}\% to \HSuppSevenBDeclineEpochThirty{}\%), while self-contamination and the non-leaked condition preserve held-out execution rate throughout training. Part of the cross-model gap therefore reflects a general capability cost specific to training on another model's outputs, not memorisation alone; the self-contamination gap carries no such confound. Read together, both results locate memorisation at the artefact rather than the benchmark. % cite:external -- "1.5" (Qwen 1.5B) is a model parameter-count identifier, not a result value

\subsection{H1 Test 1: Dynamic-L4 vs.\ ULT Shows No Directional Effect}
Restricting the dynamic benchmark to its highest complexity tier (L4, cyclomatic complexity ${\geq}8$) matches ULT's own complexity floor, giving a complexity-controlled comparison across \HOneTOneN{} of the nine canonical models (Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct excluded, a genuine floor effect: zero baseline-passing L4 completions). A paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test gives a rank-biserial effect size of \HOneTOneEffectR{} ($p=\HOneTOneP{}$): the rank-weighted pattern leans toward the dynamic benchmark scoring \emph{higher} than ULT, opposite to H1's own prediction, but far too small and noisy at $n=8$ to be distinguishable from no effect. Pairing is conducted at model level rather than the approved wording's literal model--problem level, a structural necessity rather than a simplification: the dynamic benchmark and ULT are generated from disjoint sources and share no common problem identity to pair on. \textbf{H1 Test 1 is not supported.} This null result is informative rather than merely inconclusive. One alternative explanation for H2's finding is that the dynamic benchmark is simply a harder task at matched complexity. H1 Test 1 rules that out: models with no deliberate contamination score comparably on both benchmarks. H2's near-zero dynamic gain therefore reflects an absence of contamination, not a difficulty or ceiling artefact. % cite:external -- "1.5" is the model's own parameter-count identifier, not a result value

\subsection{H1 Test 2: PLT Scores Higher Than ULT, Direction-Consistent but Correction-Dependent}
ULT and its leaked twin PLT are compared directly, CC-band matched, across all nine canonical models. PLT scores higher than ULT under both conditionings tested -- rank-biserial effect size \HOneTTwoEffectR{} under the primary reading, \HOneTTwoAdjEffectR{} under the co-primary -- the contamination-consistent direction, since PLT is by construction the leaked variant of the same problems.

Two conditionings were fixed in advance and are reported together. The primary reading follows the approved hypothesis wording: mutation score conditional on baseline correctness, averaged only over problems where the generated suite already passes against the reference implementation -- this study's pre-registered primary metric throughout. The co-primary reading restricts the comparison to a zero-filled, unconditional reading, in which a suite that fails the baseline gate scores zero rather than being excluded -- matching ULT/PLT's own source paper's \emph{stated prose formula}, though not the paper's own published scoring code, which implements the conditional/primary reading instead (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). Within RQ1's two-test Holm-Bonferroni family (H1 Test 1 and H1 Test 2 jointly), the primary reading does not survive correction ($p=\HOneTTwoP{}\rightarrow\HOneTTwoRawHolmP{}$), while the co-primary reading does ($p=\HOneTTwoAdjP{}\rightarrow\HOneTTwoAdjHolmP{}$).

\textbf{H1 Test 2 is mixed.} Direction agreement across every conditioning is the robust claim; whether it clears significance depends on which conditioning and correction is quoted, and both should be reported together rather than either alone.

\subsection{H3: Mutation-Killing Effectiveness Declines Monotonically With Tier}
\label{sec:results-h3}
Spearman correlation between ordinal tier index and mutation score is negative and significant for all nine canonical models, surviving Holm-Bonferroni correction within both the all-tier family (\HThreeAllTierSig{}) and a ceiling-robustness family excluding the near-ceiling L1 tier (\HThreeLTwoLFourSig{} computable models). The approved hypothesis's stronger, literal clause -- that mean mutation score never increases between adjacent tiers -- holds exactly for seven of the nine models; two (GPT-5 and GPT-5-mini) each show a single upward step at the L1$\rightarrow$L2 transition, both under one percentage point and both already at or above 98\% mutation score at L1, consistent with near-ceiling measurement noise rather than a substantive reversal. \textbf{H3 is supported as a monotone trend.} The literal non-rebound clause holds for seven of nine models.

\subsection{H5: Score-Level Stability Is Strong, Rank-Level Stability Falls Short}
\label{sec:results-h5}
Two properties are evaluated separately rather than blended into a single verdict. Each model's own aggregate mutation score is highly stable across re-instantiations: standard deviation \HFiveSdRange{} for most canonical models. Pairwise Kendall's $\tau$ between per-instantiation model rankings is markedly weaker: mean $\tau=\HFiveNFourTau{}$ at the four-instantiation headline scope, and $\tau=\HFiveAllPairsTau{}$ across all pairs including a partial fifth instantiation, both below the approved \HFiveTauThreshold{} threshold. Only \HFiveNFourSigCount{} of the six headline-scope pairs is individually significant at raw $p<\SigLevel{}$, and \HFiveNFourHolmCount{} survives Holm correction. The shortfall reflects the model population, not an unreliable instrument. Several canonical models' pooled scores sit closer together than their own between-instantiation standard deviation. No ranking instrument could separate models that close from this data alone. Reaching the pre-registered fifth instantiation for the two remaining models (both from one provider) was costed directly against this diagnosis and declined, since the same clustering predicts further data would not close the gap. \textbf{H5's threshold is not met}: score-level reliability is strong and not in question; rank-level stability, as specified, falls short.

\subsection{H6: Construct Validity Against an External Proxy}
Three benchmark pairs are relevant here, and are reported separately: Dynamic$\leftrightarrow$BigCodeBench, the pair H6 clause 1 is actually specified against; ULT$\leftrightarrow$BigCodeBench, the clause-2 comparator; and Dynamic$\leftrightarrow$ULT, additional context from this project's own two instruments rather than either pre-specified pair.

\textbf{Dynamic$\leftrightarrow$BigCodeBench (clause 1).} The three-model canonical reading (Qwen only) is a perfect correlation ($\tau=\HSixCanonRawTau{}$) at both metrics, but this is a within-family scaling artefact, not a validity result: three same-family models of increasing size rank identically on almost any capability measure. A four-model cross-family extension, added to break this confound, gives the honest reading at seven models: raw conditional agreement $\tau=\HSixDynBcbRawTau{}$ (not individually significant at $n=7$), coverage-adjusted agreement $\tau=\HSixDynBcbAdjTau{}$ ($p=\HSixDynBcbAdjHolmP{}$ Holm-adjusted, survives correction), shown in Figure~\ref{fig:collapse}. Against the pre-specified \HSixBandLow{}--\HSixBandHigh{} validity-supporting band, the primary reading's point estimate falls \emph{inside} the band, though the test itself does not reach significance at this sample size; the co-primary reading exceeds the band, raising a redundancy concern under the same pre-specified criterion. \textbf{H6 clause 1 is mixed}: positive under both metrics, and on its primary reading landing inside the band the pre-registration anticipated, but the co-primary reading is strong enough to suggest the two measures may be closer to redundant than ``related but distinct'' implies.

Two of the seven cross-family points are individually thin-cell by this study's own $n_{\text{defined}}{<}20$ threshold (one model's ULT-side sample: \HSixCodeLlamaUltNDefined{} defined rows; another's dynamic- and ULT-side samples: \HSixMistralDynNDefined{} and \HSixMistralUltNDefined{}), a caveat that travels with the cross-family figures.

\textbf{Dynamic$\leftrightarrow$ULT (context, not part of the approved test).} The same two metrics can also be computed between the dynamic benchmark and ULT directly ($n=9$ canonical models), since both are this project's own instruments -- not one of H6's two pre-specified BigCodeBench pairs, but relevant context for how much the project's own benchmarks agree with each other. Agreement is weaker under the conditional metric ($\tau=\HSixCondTau{}$, not significant) and much stronger under the coverage-adjusted metric ($\tau=\HSixAdjTau{}$, Holm $p=\HSixAdjHolmP{}$). Decomposing the coverage-adjusted figure shows baseline-pass-rate agreement alone already reaches $\tau=\HSixBpTau{}$: most of the apparent Dynamic--ULT agreement reflects which models can produce an executing, passing suite at all, not which suites kill more mutants once that is controlled for. Excluding every repaired row leaves the model ranking unchanged from the baseline-pass-only ranking, so this robustness variant returns the same $\tau=\HSixSanitizerWithoutTau{}$.

\textbf{ULT$\leftrightarrow$BigCodeBench (clause 2), vs.\ Dynamic$\leftrightarrow$BigCodeBench.} Clause 2, whether the dynamic-proxy correlation is weaker than the static-proxy correlation, is answerable directly from data already computed for clause 1: the static (ULT) correlation with BigCodeBench is $\tau=\HSixUltBcbRawTau{}$ raw and $\tau=\HSixUltBcbAdjTau{}$ coverage-adjusted, both weaker than the dynamic benchmark's own $\HSixDynBcbRawTau{}$ and $\HSixDynBcbAdjTau{}$. \textbf{H6 clause 2 is not supported.} The dynamic-proxy correlation is the stronger one under every statistic and metric definition tested, opposite to the pre-registered prediction. Stated plainly, the dynamic benchmark tracks external code-generation capability at least as well as, and by most measures better than, the static benchmark it was designed as a contamination-resistant alternative to. \subsection{H4: Declared Untestable by Construction}
The approved hypothesis predicts a larger static-versus-dynamic score gap at low complexity tiers (L1--L2) than at high ones (L3--L4). ULT and PLT contain no problems below cyclomatic complexity eight, a filter applied by their own source authors, so no static-benchmark score exists at L1--L2 to form the predicted gap with. \textbf{H4 is dropped as untestable by construction.} The decision was taken with supervisor sign-off rather than reshaping the hypothesis post hoc; the reason itself is informative, since a complexity-filtered static benchmark cannot pose the question the hypothesis asks. RQ3 is instead answered narratively by H3's within-dynamic-benchmark tier gradient (Section~\ref{sec:results-h3}) together with this structural finding.
