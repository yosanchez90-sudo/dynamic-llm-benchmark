\subsection{H2: Fine-Tuning Inflates the Static Benchmark, Not DyMuT}
\label{sec:results-h2}
Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct, LoRA-fine-tuned directly on ULT's own repair-verified ground truth (\HTwoSeenN{} problems), gains \HTwoSeenGain{} percentage points (95\% CI \HTwoSeenGainCI{}) on that same seen-ULT split, with \HTwoPreMutkTotal{} of \HTwoPreMutkTotal{} post-fine-tune completions byte-identical to their training target - direct evidence of memorisation. A control condition measures how much the model improved at test writing in general: the DyMuT tiers L1--L3, which the fine-tuning run never trained on, move by only \HTwoLOneLThreeGain{} points (CI \HTwoLOneLThreeGainCI{}). The complexity-matched DyMuT control (L4) moves by exactly \HTwoLFourGain{} points. PLT, the ULT's leaked twin, was evaluated on the same checkpoint. After subtracting the L1--L3 control, it gains \HTwoPltDid{} points (CI \HTwoPltDidCI{}), smaller than the seen-ULT gain but clearly non-zero. The fine-tuning run never saw PLT directly, so this indicates that leaked content confers an indirect memorisation benefit. % cite:external -- "1.5" is the model's own parameter-count identifier (Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct), not a result value

Unseen-ULT itself gains \HTwoUnseenGain{} points with no control subtracted; net of the L1--L3 control's own \HTwoLOneLThreeGain{}-point generic-improvement gain, roughly \HTwoUnseenNetOfControl{} points of that gain sit within the static benchmark on problems this fine-tuning run never directly trained on -- transfer to problems sharing ULT's distribution, not memorisation of a specific answer, and worth noting rather than left silent.

\subsection{H2-Supplementary: Contamination Within DyMuT}
\label{sec:results-h2supp}
This probe sits outside the six pre-registered hypotheses. It asks what happens when a single instantiation is leaked into training. Does the inflation attach to the leaked artefact, or to the benchmark as a whole? Two Qwen models (1.5B, 7B) were each fine-tuned three ways on instantiation 1: on their own prior generations restricted to that instantiation (self-contamination), on a stronger model's generations on the same instantiation (cross-model contamination, GPT-5), and, as a no-leak control, on instantiation 1's reference outputs filtered only by whether they execute rather than whether they are correct. Both leaked conditions show a seen-versus-fresh gap in the expected direction; the no-leak control shows none, within noise. Held-out execution rate, whether the generated suite runs at all, declines by epoch 30 for both cross-model-trained checkpoints (1.5B: \HSuppOneFiveBDeclineEpochZero{}\% to \HSuppOneFiveBDeclineEpochThirty{}\%; 7B: \HSuppSevenBDeclineEpochZero{}\% to \HSuppSevenBDeclineEpochThirty{}\%), while self-contamination and the no-leak control preserve held-out execution rate throughout training. This decline appears in both cross-model conditions and in neither self-contamination condition: cross-model contamination degrades general capability as well as inducing memorisation, while self-contamination induces memorisation without the same capability cost. Part of the cross-model gap therefore reflects a general capability cost specific to training on another model's outputs, not memorisation alone; the self-contamination gap carries no such confound. Read together, both results locate memorisation at the artefact rather than the benchmark. % cite:external -- "1.5" (Qwen 1.5B) is a model parameter-count identifier, not a result value

\subsection{H1 Test 1: DyMuT-L4 vs.\ ULT Shows No Directional Effect}
Restricting DyMuT to its highest complexity tier (L4, cyclomatic complexity ${\geq}8$) matches ULT's own complexity floor, giving a complexity-controlled comparison across \HOneTOneN{} of the nine canonical models (Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct excluded as zero baseline-passing L4 completions). A paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test gave a rank-biserial effect size of \HOneTOneEffectR{} ($p=\HOneTOneP{}$): the rank-weighted pattern leaned toward DyMuT scoring \emph{higher} than ULT, opposite to H1's own prediction. Pairing was conducted at model level rather than the approved wording's literal model-problem level: DyMuT and ULT are generated from disjoint sources and share no common problem identity to pair on. \textbf{H1 Test 1 is not supported.} This null result is informative. One alternative explanation for H2's finding is that DyMuT is a harder task at matched complexity. H1 Test 1 rules that out: models with no deliberate contamination score comparably on both benchmarks. H2's near-zero DyMuT gain therefore reflects an absence of contamination. % cite:external -- "1.5" is the model's own parameter-count identifier, not a result value

\subsection{H1 Test 2: PLT Scores Higher Than ULT, Direction-Consistent but Correction-Dependent}
ULT and its leaked twin PLT were compared directly, CC-band matched, across all nine canonical models. PLT scored higher than ULT under both conditionings tested, rank-biserial effect size \HOneTTwoEffectR{} under the primary reading, \HOneTTwoAdjEffectR{} under the co-primary, the contamination-consistent direction, since PLT is by construction the leaked variant of the same problems.

Two conditionings were fixed in advance and are reported together: mutation score - conditional on baseline correctness, averaged only over problems where the generated suite already passes against the reference implementation. The co-primary reading is unconditional. A suite that fails the baseline gate scores zero instead of being excluded from the average, matching ULT/PLT's own source paper's \emph{stated prose formula}, though not the paper's own published scoring code, which implements the conditional reading instead. Within RQ1's two-test Holm-Bonferroni~\cite{holm1979simple} family, the primary reading did not survive correction ($p=\HOneTTwoP{}\rightarrow\HOneTTwoRawHolmP{}$), while the co-primary reading did ($p=\HOneTTwoAdjP{}\rightarrow\HOneTTwoAdjHolmP{}$).

\textbf{H1 Test 2 is mixed.} Direction agreement across every conditioning is the robust claim; whether it clears significance depends on which conditioning and correction is quoted, and both are reported together rather than alone.

\subsection{H3: Mutation-Killing Effectiveness Declines Monotonically With Tier}
\label{sec:results-h3}
Spearman correlation between tier index and mutation score was negative and significant for all nine canonical models, surviving Holm-Bonferroni correction within both the all-tier family (\HThreeAllTierSig{}) and a ceiling-robustness family excluding the near-ceiling L1 tier (\HThreeLTwoLFourSig{} computable models). The approved hypothesis's literal clause, that mean mutation score never increases between adjacent tiers, held exactly for seven of the nine models; two (GPT-5 and GPT-5-mini) each showed a single upward step at the L1$\rightarrow$L2 transition, both under one percentage point and both at or above 98\% mutation score at L1, consistent with near-ceiling measurement noise. \textbf{H3 is supported as a monotone trend.} The strict condition of no score increases held for seven of nine models.

\subsection{H5: Score-Level Stability Is Strong, Rank-Level Stability Falls Short}
\label{sec:results-h5}
Two properties are evaluated separately. Each model's own aggregate mutation score was highly stable across re-instantiations: standard deviation \HFiveSdRange{} for most canonical models. Pairwise Kendall's $\tau$ between per-instantiation model rankings was markedly weaker: mean $\tau=\HFiveNFourTau{}$ at the four-instantiation headline scope, and $\tau=\HFiveAllPairsTau{}$ across all pairs including a partial fifth instantiation, both below the approved \HFiveTauThreshold{} threshold. \HFiveNFourSigCount{} of the six headline-scope pairs were individually significant at raw $p<\SigLevel{}$, and \HFiveNFourHolmCount{} survived Holm correction. The shortfall reflects the model population, not an unreliable instrument. Several canonical models' pooled scores sat closer than their own between-instantiation standard deviation. No ranking instrument could separate models that close from this data alone. Reaching the pre-registered fifth instantiation for the two remaining models was costed against this diagnosis and declined, since the same clustering predicts further data would not close the gap. \textbf{H5's threshold is not met}: score-level reliability is strong and not in question; rank-level stability, as specified, falls short.

\subsection{H6: Construct Validity Against an External Proxy}
Three benchmark pairs are relevant here, and are reported separately: DyMuT$\leftrightarrow$BigCodeBench, the pair H6 clause 1 is specified against; ULT$\leftrightarrow$BigCodeBench, the clause-2 comparator; and DyMuT$\leftrightarrow$ULT.

\textbf{DyMuT$\leftrightarrow$BigCodeBench (clause 1).} The three-model canonical reading (Qwen only) was a perfect correlation ($\tau=\HSixCanonRawTau{}$) at both metrics, but this is a within-family scaling artefact, not a validity result: three same-family models of increasing size rank identically on almost any capability measure. A four-model cross-family extension, added to break this confound, gave the honest reading at seven models: raw conditional agreement $\tau=\HSixDynBcbRawTau{}$, coverage-adjusted agreement $\tau=\HSixDynBcbAdjTau{}$ ($p=\HSixDynBcbAdjHolmP{}$ Holm-adjusted, survived correction), shown in Figure~\ref{fig:collapse}. Against the pre-specified \HSixBandLow{}-\HSixBandHigh{} validity-supporting band, the primary reading's point estimate fell \emph{inside} the band, though the test did not reach significance at this sample size; the co-primary reading exceeded the band. \textbf{H6 clause 1 is mixed}: positive under both metrics, and on its primary reading landing inside the band the pre-registration anticipated, but the co-primary reading exceeds it, a consequence of that metric's baseline-pass dominance rather than true redundancy.

Two of the seven cross-family points are individually thin-cell by this study's own $n_{\text{defined}}{<}20$ threshold (one model's ULT reading, \HSixCodeLlamaUltNDefined{} defined rows; another's DyMuT and ULT readings, \HSixMistralDynNDefined{} and \HSixMistralUltNDefined{} defined rows respectively), a caveat that travels with the cross-family figures.

\textbf{DyMuT$\leftrightarrow$ULT.} The same two metrics can be computed between DyMuT and ULT directly ($n=9$ canonical models), since both are this project's own instruments, not one of H6's two pre-specified BigCodeBench pairs, but relevant context for how much the project's own benchmarks agree with each other. Agreement was weaker under the conditional metric ($\tau=\HSixCondTau{}$, not significant) and much stronger under the coverage-adjusted metric ($\tau=\HSixAdjTau{}$, Holm $p=\HSixAdjHolmP{}$). Decomposing the coverage-adjusted figure showed baseline-pass-rate agreement alone reached $\tau=\HSixBpTau{}$: most of the apparent DyMuT--ULT agreement reflects which models can produce an executing, passing suite, not which suites kill more mutants. Excluding every repaired row left the model ranking unchanged from the baseline-pass-only ranking, so this robustness variant returned the same $\tau=\HSixSanitizerWithoutTau{}$.

\textbf{ULT$\leftrightarrow$BigCodeBench (clause 2).} Clause 2 asks whether the DyMuT-proxy correlation is weaker than the static-proxy correlation. It can be answered from the data already computed for clause 1: the static (ULT) correlation with BigCodeBench was $\tau=\HSixUltBcbRawTau{}$ raw and $\tau=\HSixUltBcbAdjTau{}$ coverage-adjusted, both weaker than DyMuT's own $\HSixDynBcbRawTau{}$ and $\HSixDynBcbAdjTau{}$. \textbf{H6 clause 2 is not supported.} The DyMuT-proxy correlation was the stronger one under every statistic and metric definition tested. This is the opposite of the pre-registered prediction. DyMuT tracks external code-generation capability better than the static benchmark does. \subsection{H4: Declared Untestable by Construction}
The approved hypothesis predicts a larger static-versus-DyMuT score gap at low complexity tiers (L1--L2) than at high ones (L3--L4). ULT and PLT contain no problems below cyclomatic complexity eight, a filter applied by their own source authors, so no static-benchmark score exists at L1--L2 to form the predicted gap with. \textbf{H4 is dropped as untestable by construction}, since a complexity-filtered static benchmark cannot pose the question the hypothesis asks.
