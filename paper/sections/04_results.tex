\section{Experimentation and Results}
\label{sec:results}

Six pre-registered hypotheses were evaluated against the criteria fixed before any result was computed; a supplementary probe extends H2's design. Table~\ref{tab:hypotheses} summarises every prediction and verdict; the order the paper's discussion follows differs from the order of the research questions, since H2's causal result frames how the more mixed observational results should be read. The supporting report tabulates every canonical model's raw scores on both benchmarks, across every tier and metric, before any hypothesis test below is applied to that same data.

\subsection{H2: Fine-Tuning Inflates the Static Benchmark, Not DyMuT (Comparison C)}
\label{sec:results-h2}
Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B, LoRA-fine-tuned on ULT's repair-verified ground truth (\HTwoSeenN{} problems), gains \HTwoSeenGain{} percentage points (95\% CI \HTwoSeenGainCI{}) on that same seen-ULT split. All \HTwoPreMutkTotal{} post-fine-tune completions are byte-identical to their training target, which is direct evidence of memorisation. Two controls measure what else changed. The general-improvement control, DyMuT tiers L1--L3, which the fine-tuning never trained on, moves by \HTwoLOneLThreeGain{} points (CI \HTwoLOneLThreeGainCI{}). The complexity-matched control, DyMuT L4, moves by exactly \HTwoLFourGain{} points. PLT was evaluated on the same checkpoint. After subtracting the L1--L3 control it gains \HTwoPltDid{} points (CI \HTwoPltDidCI{}), smaller than the seen-ULT gain but clearly non-zero. The fine-tuning never saw PLT directly, so leaked content gives an indirect memorisation benefit. % cite:external -- "1.5" is the model's own parameter-count identifier (Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct), not a result value

\subsection{H1 Test 1: DyMuT-L4 vs.\ ULT Shows No Directional Effect (Comparison A)}
\label{sec:results-h1test1}
Restricting DyMuT to its highest complexity tier (L4, cyclomatic complexity ${\geq}8$) matches ULT's own complexity floor, giving a complexity-controlled comparison across \HOneTOneN{} of the nine models (Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct excluded as zero baseline-passing L4 completions). One of these eight, Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview, contributes its ULT side from an incomplete crawl (101 of 400 \texttt{core\_400} problems), a pre-existing gap also reaching H6 clause 2 below (Section~\ref{sec:results-h6clause2}). A paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test gave a rank-biserial effect size of \HOneTOneEffectR{} ($p=\HOneTOneP{}$): the rank-weighted pattern leaned toward DyMuT scoring \emph{higher} than ULT, opposite to H1's prediction, paired at model level since DyMuT and ULT share no common problem identity. \textbf{H1 Test 1 is not supported.} This null result is informative: it provides no evidence that DyMuT is simply a harder task at matched complexity, an alternative explanation for H2's finding, since models with no deliberate contamination score comparably on both benchmarks, so H2's near-zero DyMuT gain reflects an absence of contamination. % cite:external -- "1.5" is the model's own parameter-count identifier, not a result value

\subsection{H1 Test 2: PLT Scores Higher Than ULT, Direction-Consistent but Correction-Dependent (Comparison B)}
\label{sec:results-h1test2}
ULT and its leaked twin PLT were compared directly, CC-band matched, across all nine canonical models. PLT scored higher than ULT under both conditionings tested (mentioned below), rank-biserial effect size \HOneTTwoEffectR{} under the primary reading, \HOneTTwoAdjEffectR{} under the co-primary, consistent with the presence of contamination, since PLT is the leaked variant of the same problems.

Two conditionings were established in advance and are reported together: mutation score conditional on baseline correctness (averaged only over problems whose suite already passes against the reference implementation), and an unconditional co-primary reading, where a suite failing the baseline gate scores zero instead of being excluded, matching ULT/PLT's paper's \emph{stated prose formula} though not its published scoring code, which implements the conditional reading instead. Within RQ1's two-test Holm-Bonferroni~\cite{holm1979simple} family, the primary reading did not survive correction ($p=\HOneTTwoP{}\rightarrow\HOneTTwoRawHolmP{}$), while the co-primary did ($p=\HOneTTwoAdjP{}\rightarrow\HOneTTwoAdjHolmP{}$).

\textbf{H1 Test 2 is mixed.} While PLT consistently outperforms ULT across all conditionings, whether this difference clears significance depends on which conditioning and correction is quoted, and both are reported together rather than independently.

Holm-Bonferroni~\cite{holm1979simple} correction is applied within five separate families throughout this section, never pooled across them; the supporting report tabulates all five.

\subsection{H3: Mutation-Killing Effectiveness Declines Monotonically With Tier}
\label{sec:results-h3}
Spearman correlation between tier index and mutation score was negative and significant for all nine canonical models, surviving Holm-Bonferroni correction within both the all-tier family (\HThreeAllTierSig{} computable models) and a ceiling-robustness family excluding the near-ceiling L1 tier (\HThreeLTwoLFourSig{} computable models; Qwen 1.5B is the one model excluded, for insufficient L4 tier range). The approved hypothesis's literal clause, that mean mutation score never increases between adjacent tiers, held exactly for seven of the nine models; two (GPT-5 and GPT-5-mini) each showed a single upward step at the L1$\rightarrow$L2 transition, both under one percentage point and both at or above 98\% mutation score at L1, consistent with near-ceiling measurement noise. \textbf{H3 is supported as a monotone trend.} The strict condition of no score increases held for seven of nine models: monotone for the three open-source Qwen sizes, much more gradual for the six commercial models, which stay closer to ceiling through L3 (Figure~\ref{fig:h3tiereffect} plots this by model). % cite:external -- "1.5" (Qwen 1.5B) is a model parameter-count identifier, not a result value

\subsection{H5: Score-Level Stability Is Strong, Rank-Level Stability Falls Short}
\label{sec:results-h5}
Two separate properties were evaluated: score reliability (how consistent a model's own score is) and rank stability (how consistent the relative order of models is across independent re-instantiations). Individual scores are highly stable: when re-instantiating the same model across independent benchmark regenerations, its mutation score barely changed, standard deviation \HFiveSdRange{} for most canonical models. Model rankings are less stable: pairwise Kendall's $\tau$ between per-instantiation model rankings was markedly weaker, mean $\tau=\HFiveNFourTau{}$ across the primary four instantiations, and $\tau=\HFiveAllPairsTau{}$ when adding a partial fifth instantiation, both below the approved \HFiveTauThreshold{} threshold. \HFiveNFourSigCount{} of the six headline-scope pairs were individually significant at raw $p<\SigLevel{}$, and \HFiveNFourHolmCount{} survived Holm correction. This shortfall reflects the model population, not an unreliable instrument: several canonical models' pooled scores sat closer than their own between-instantiation standard deviation, so no ranking instrument could separate them from this data alone. Completing the pre-registered fifth instantiation for the two remaining models (both Gemini) was costed and, for two reasons, not completed: no realistic result from that additional data crosses the $\HFiveNFourTau{}\rightarrow\HFiveTauThreshold{}$ gap, and the one time more data actually landed for this comparison (Gemini Pro's own L4 data), $\tau$ moved further down, from 0.667 to \HFiveNFourTau{}, evidence that the tight clustering is a real property of these models, not something more data would resolve. \textbf{H5's threshold was not met}: score-level reliability is strong and not in question; rank-level stability, as specified, falls short. % cite:historical -- 0.667 is the pre-L4-landing headline tau this project reported before 2026-08-11, superseded by \HFiveNFourTau{} once Gemini Pro's L4 data completed; kept here as the historical evidence that more data lowered, not raised, agreement

\subsection{H6 Clause 1: DyMuT$\leftrightarrow$BigCodeBench Agreement Is Mixed}
\label{sec:results-h6clause1}
Three benchmark pairs are relevant to H6's construct-validity check, and are reported separately: DyMuT$\leftrightarrow$BigCodeBench (this clause); ULT$\leftrightarrow$BigCodeBench (clause 2, Section~\ref{sec:results-h6clause2}); and DyMuT$\leftrightarrow$ULT, relevant context reported alongside clause 2. The three-model canonical reading (Qwen only) was a perfect correlation ($\tau=\HSixCanonRawTau{}$) at both metrics, but this is a within-family scaling artefact, not a validity result: three same-family models of increasing size rank identically on almost any capability measure. A four-model cross-family extension, added to break this confound, gave the honest reading at seven models: raw conditional agreement collapses to $\tau=\HSixDynBcbRawTau{}$ ($p=\HSixDynBcbRawP{}$, n.s.), while coverage-adjusted agreement survives at $\tau=\HSixDynBcbAdjTau{}$ ($p=\HSixDynBcbAdjRawP{}\rightarrow\HSixDynBcbAdjHolmP{}$ raw$\rightarrow$Holm). Against the pre-specified \HSixBandLow{}-\HSixBandHigh{} validity-supporting band, the primary reading's point estimate fell \emph{inside} the band, though the test did not reach significance at this sample size; the co-primary reading exceeded the band. \textbf{H6 clause 1 is mixed}: positive under both metrics, and on its primary reading landing inside the band the pre-registration anticipated, but the co-primary reading exceeds it, a consequence of that metric's baseline-pass dominance rather than redundancy. The supporting report plots this collapse-versus-survival pattern directly.

These seven cross-family points are unevenly thin ($n_{\text{defined}}{<}20$): on the DyMuT side only Mistral-7B is thin (\HSixMistralDynNDefined{} rows), but on the ULT side four of seven are -- CodeLlama-13B (\HSixCodeLlamaUltNDefined{}), Qwen 7B (\HSixQwenSevenBUltNDefined{}), Qwen 1.5B (\HSixQwenOneFiveBUltNDefined{}), and Mistral-7B (\HSixMistralUltNDefined{}) rows; the supporting report gives a thin-cell-excluded robustness recompute for both pairs. % cite:external -- "1.5" (Qwen 1.5B) is a model parameter-count identifier, not a result value

\subsection{H6 Clause 2: DyMuT Tracks the External Proxy Better Than ULT Does}
\label{sec:results-h6clause2}
Clause 2 asks whether the DyMuT-proxy correlation is weaker than the ULT-proxy correlation. The static (ULT) correlation with BigCodeBench was $\tau=\HSixUltBcbRawTau{}$ raw and $\tau=\HSixUltBcbAdjTau{}$ coverage-adjusted, both weaker than DyMuT's own $\HSixDynBcbRawTau{}$ and $\HSixDynBcbAdjTau{}$. \textbf{H6 clause 2 is not supported.} The DyMuT-proxy correlation was stronger under every statistic and metric definition tested. This is the opposite of the pre-registered prediction. DyMuT tracks external code-generation capability better than the static benchmark does.

\textbf{DyMuT$\leftrightarrow$ULT.} The same two metrics can be computed between DyMuT and ULT directly ($n=9$ canonical models), relevant context for how much the project's own benchmarks agree with each other. Agreement was weaker under the conditional metric ($\tau=\HSixCondTau{}$, not significant) and much stronger under the coverage-adjusted metric ($\tau=\HSixAdjTau{}$, Holm $p=\HSixAdjHolmP{}$). Decomposing the coverage-adjusted figure showed \texttt{baseline\_pass\_rate} agreement reached $\tau=\HSixBpTau{}$: most of the apparent DyMuT--ULT agreement reflects which models can produce an executing, passing suite, not which suites kill more mutants. Excluding every repaired row left the model ranking unchanged from the baseline-pass-only ranking, so this robustness variant returned the same $\tau=\HSixSanitizerWithoutTau{}$. Figure~\ref{fig:rankingmatrixcrossfamily} summarises rank agreement across all three pairs and four metrics, at its own wider 13-model scope for DyMuT$\leftrightarrow$ULT.

\subsection{H4: Static-Dynamic Gap at Low Complexity Tiers}
\label{sec:results-h4}
The approved hypothesis predicts a larger static-versus-DyMuT score gap at low complexity tiers (L1--L2) than at high ones (L3--L4). ULT and PLT contain no problems below cyclomatic complexity eight, a filter applied by their own source authors, so no static-benchmark score exists at L1--L2 to form the predicted gap with. \textbf{H4 is dropped as untestable by construction}, since this static benchmark cannot pose the question the hypothesis asks.
