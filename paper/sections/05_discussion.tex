\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The pattern across H1--H6 is most coherently read as evidence from two distinct kinds of test: one causal, one observational, rather than as a single, uniform contamination effect. H2's causal result shows contamination as a training-time memorisation effect: the fine-tuned checkpoint reproduces its training targets verbatim and passes near-perfectly on the exact problems it trained on. H1's observational results, weaker and more equivocal by design, probe a different question, whether models trained by others, on unknown corpora, already carry this same effect for ULT's specific published content. The two results are not in tension: a causal probe with a single, controlled intervention can show a clean, large effect that an observational comparison across nine models of unknown training history cannot be expected to recover with the same clarity.

A further finding shows post-fine-tuning mutation score on the seen-ULT split, despite 100\% verbatim reproduction of the training target, is $\HTwoPostMutk{}$, not 1.0. Byte-identical recall of a reference test suite does not guarantee the recalled suite kills every mutant the scoring harness generates. This sets a ceiling on how much a purely memorisation-driven strategy can inflate a mutation-testing score, independent of whether the benchmark resists contamination at all.

The benchmark's validity argument is top-down by design: complexity tiers, mutation operators, and scoring metric are all specified in advance from an explicit model of what test-generation capability should look like. H6 supplies the complementary bottom-up check: does the benchmark's empirical ranking of models agree with an independent capability measure? The answer is mixed: the conditional primary metric's point estimate lands inside the pre-registered validity band, though not as significant at the available sample size, while the coverage-adjusted co-primary exceeds it, decomposing to reveal that most of the latter agreement is really agreement about executability rather than about mutation-testing quality specifically. Neither reading alone settles the question; together they place the benchmark closer to its intended target, correlated with general code-generation capability, without being reducible to it.

Several threats to validity apply. Executing untrusted, template-generated or scraped ground-truth code in a subprocess: an early ground-truth quality check that executed thousands of real-world ULT functions with the working directory left at the project root produced several stray files from functions performing ordinary relative-path I/O as a side effect, a lesson since fixed by always executing untrusted code from an isolated temporary showing the gap between "runs in a subprocess" and "runs safely. The causal fine-tuning result (H2) uses a single model at a single, small parameter scale; whether the same clean separation between memorisation and transfer holds at larger scale is untested here. Mutation score throughout is conditional on baseline correctness by design (Section~\ref{sec:methods-metrics}), which Section~\ref{sec:results} shows materially affects cross-benchmark rank agreement (H6) and should be read as a property of the specific metric definition rather than of the benchmark's validity. ULT's own published ground truth passes this study's harness at a lower rate than its role as a correctness reference would suggest, a finding corroborated by an internal recomputation of the benchmark's own complexity labels and by a direct contradiction between the source paper's stated mutation-score formula and its own published scoring code: this study's co-primary (coverage-adjusted) definition matches the source paper's prose formula, while this study's primary (conditional) definition matches the paper's own published code instead, which is why both are reported as co-primaries throughout. None of this study's reported metrics are affected by the contradiction, since it is disclosed and accounted for; the finding is reported here as a caveat on ULT's suitability as a ground-truth reference.
