Benchmark contamination -- the presence of evaluation data in a large language model's (LLM) training corpus, inflates reported scores on static test suites in ways difficult to detect after the fact. This work evaluates whether a dynamic, template-based benchmark for LLM unit-test generation resists this inflation more effectively than a state-of-the-art static alternative. Sixty parameterised templates spanning four cyclomatic-complexity tiers generate fresh, seeded problem instances at evaluation time, scored by a seven-operator mutation-testing suite rather than fixed reference tests. Nine models across four providers are evaluated against the dynamic benchmark. A decontaminated static baseline (ULT), and a small open-source model is fine-tuned on that baseline's ground truth to probe contamination sensitivity causally. The fine-tuned model gains \HTwoSeenGain{} percentage points on the memorised static split against an \HTwoLOneLThreeGain{}-point generic-improvement control, while its dynamic-benchmark score moves by exactly zero, the clearest evidence that it rejects contamination the static one absorbs. Six hypotheses were pre-specified and evaluated under Holm-Bonferroni correction where p-values apply; the causal contamination-resistance evidence holds throughout. One pre-specified directional prediction was contradicted, the dynamic benchmark tracks external code-generation ability more closely than the static benchmark, plus one comparison was not distinguishable from no effect, and one stability threshold was not met. The findings support dynamic, template-based generation as a practical, contamination-resistant evaluation strategy for LLM test-generation research, while identifying where its statistical power falls short of a pre-registered bar.
