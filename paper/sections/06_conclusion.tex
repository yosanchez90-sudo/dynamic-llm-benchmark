\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work examined whether a dynamic, template-based benchmark for LLM unit-test generation resists contamination-induced score inflation more effectively than a decontaminated static alternative, without losing the ability to measure genuine test-generation capability. The clearest evidence obtained remains the causal result reported in Section~\ref{sec:results-h2}. The dynamic benchmark also tracks an external code-generation proxy at least as well as the static benchmark does, addressing the risk that contamination resistance came at the cost of construct validity. Taken together, the six pre-registered hypotheses support dynamic, template-based generation as a practical, contamination-resistant evaluation strategy for LLM test-generation research, while identifying where its present statistical power (principally in ranking stability across independent re-instantiations) falls short of a pre-registered bar.

Several directions were scoped out of this study and remain open: a sensitivity sweep at five attempts per problem and a one-shot prompting condition, both cut first under budget pressure with no experimental work begun; and a fifth independent benchmark instantiation for the two models that did not reach it, whose cost of completion was quantified directly and declined on diagnostic grounds (Section~\ref{sec:results-h5}). Semantic-preserving-change mutation operators, complementary to the semantic-altering-change operators used throughout this study, would extend the mutation-testing methodology to a robustness dimension not evaluated here.
