## Diagnosis (post-hoc, `analysis/RQ5_DIAGNOSTICS.md` has the full tables)

Four candidate explanations for τ=0.333 (updated 2026-08-07 from 0.143),
checked in turn. **The pooled
result above remains the pre-registered primary and is not superseded by
anything below** — this is a diagnosis of why it came out comparatively
low, not a higher, more flattering number to report instead.

**(a) Difficulty composition.** Pooling L1-L4 compares a mixed-difficulty
benchmark against ULT's CC>=8-only band. Tier-matching (Dynamic-L4 only,
Comparison A's own convention) does not simply weaken the correlation.
**Updated 2026-08-12, n_models=8** (unchanged count, but Gemini Pro
Preview's Dynamic-L4 crawl completing 2026-08-11 moved its own L4 mean
substantially, 38→292 defined rows): Pearson r=-0.452 (CI [-0.89, 0.99]),
Kendall τ — the pre-registered primary statistic — is **0.143** (CI
[-0.43, 1.00], perm p=0.7185). **This has itself moved from the
2026-08-07 reading immediately below**, which found τ collapsing to
exactly 0.000 — it no longer does; the CC-matched reading is now
positive, matching the pooled reading's sign rather than diverging from
it. Read precisely: this was never "tier-matching makes Dynamic and ULT
actively disagree" (that would need a negative rank correlation) at any
snapshot — it has moved from "removes whatever positive rank signal
pooling was contributing, leaving literally none" (2026-08-07) to
"recovers a small positive signal, similar in magnitude to the pooled
reading" (current). The CC-matched comparison's rank agreement (τ=0.143)
is now roughly on par with the pooled one (τ=0.278), not worse than it as
the 2026-08-07 reading suggested. The discrimination audit explains part
of why pooling is misleading regardless of sign: L1 has a 0.017 range
across all 9 models (everyone at ceiling) while L4 has a 0.378 range and
ULT a 0.494 range — pooling a near-zero-variance tier in with the
genuinely discriminating ones dilutes whatever signal the hard tiers
carry, in either direction.

**2026-08-07 reading (historical)**: n_models=8 (up from 5, once Gemini
Pro Preview's then-partial L4 landing first made this computable):
Pearson r=-0.476 (CI [-0.89, 0.97]), Kendall τ=0.000 (CI [-0.57, 0.91],
perm p=1.0000, the least-informative possible permutation reading) — not
the negative τ=-0.200 the earlier n=5 reading showed.

**(b) Conditioning/survivorship — the largest single factor found, and
still the largest at n_models=9 (updated 2026-08-07).** Mut@K is only
defined where `baseline_passed=True`, so each model's mean is taken over a
different-sized, self-selected subset (pooled baseline_pass_rate still
ranges roughly 45x between the extremes — Qwen 1.5B lowest, GPT-5 highest
— see `analysis/RQ5_PROVENANCE.md`'s model ranking table for exact current
values). Re-scoring with a coverage-adjusted metric (Mut@K × baseline_pass_rate,
which folds the selection back in rather than conditioning it away)
**recovers Kendall τ=0.944, Spearman ρ=0.983 (permutation p=0.0002)** — up
from 0.905/0.964/0.0034 at n=7, and still a near-total reversal from the
raw pooled result. This is the strongest single piece of evidence in this
diagnosis, but decomposing the product (`analysis/RQ5_DIAGNOSTICS.md` sec
3b) sharpens what it actually shows: **baseline_pass_rate ALONE already
gives τ=0.889** (up from 0.810) — nearly all of the recovered agreement is
models agreeing on who can produce a baseline-passing suite at all (PASSES
against the real function — a stricter, later gate than mere
exec/collection, which this project's scorer tracks separately as
`exec_ok`), not on mutation-killing quality. Conditioned Mut@K alone (the
pre-registered primary metric, same n_models=9/same models for a fair
comparison) is τ=0.333 — identical to the full-pool result, meaning models
that clear the baseline-pass bar still do **not** then agree nearly as
well on which suites are the *better* mutation killers, even though this
gap narrowed somewhat from n=7 (baseline_pass_rate-alone minus
conditional-Mut@K-alone: 0.810-0.143=0.667 at n=7, 0.889-0.333=0.556 at
n=9). So: most of the apparent Dynamic/ULT disagreement is still not that
the two benchmarks disagree about model *capability* broadly — it's still
specifically that they disagree about *baseline pass rate* comparatively
little and about *mutation-quality-conditional-on-passing* substantially
more, though less totally than the n=7 reading suggested. Mut@K and
coverage-adjusted-Mut@K are different estimands measuring different things
(respectively: "how good are the tests that already passed" vs "how good
is the model overall, including whether it clears the gate"); this result
still says the cross-benchmark agreement lives disproportionately in the
"clears the gate" component, not the "how good once it does" component —
a more precise and less flattering statement than "most of the
disagreement is conditioning" on its own. The coverage-adjusted score is
also markedly MORE reliable across Dynamic instantiations than raw Mut@K
(N=4 mean pairwise Kendall τ=1.000 vs 0.667, updated from 0.635 —
`analysis/RQ5_DIAGNOSTICS.md` sec 3c, `analysis/STABILITY_INTERPRETATION.md`'s
own correction history has the full movement) — verified not a small-n
artifact, and the same underlying fact as the validity finding:
baseline_pass_rate is apparently both the thing that varies enormously and
consistently BETWEEN models and the thing that stays comparatively stable
WITHIN a model across instantiations.

**(c) Reliability ceiling — a minor factor.** Disattenuating the observed
τ (now 0.333, up from the 0.143 this section originally disattenuated) by
Dynamic's own RQ4.1 self-consistency (mean pairwise Kendall tau, computed
live rather than a frozen constant — see `analysis/RQ5_DIAGNOSTICS.md`
sec 4 for the current figure and why it's computed live, not hard-coded)
gives a disattenuated τ that still moves only modestly from the raw
observed value. Dynamic's own noisiness is not the main reason the
cross-benchmark number is comparatively low.

**(d) Genuine construct difference — what's left over.** After (a) and (c)
are largely ruled out as the main drivers and (b) is shown to recover most
of the missing agreement, the residual "genuine distinct-capability" story
is smaller than the raw pooled result on its own would suggest. This
reframes the headline: **the pre-registered primary metric (pooled Mut@K)
genuinely does show weak rank agreement with ULT, and that finding stands,
unflattered** — but the diagnosis indicates this is substantially a property
of *how Mut@K conditions on baseline-pass*, not strong evidence that
Dynamic and ULT are measuring unrelated things. Both statements are true at
once and both belong in the write-up: the metric-as-specified underperforms
at cross-benchmark ranking, and the underlying capabilities it's built from
are not as disconnected as that number alone implies.

Also carried over from the per-tier check: Qwen 32B/7B score much higher on
ULT (0.90/0.89) than their Dynamic-L4 performance predicts (0.55/0.54,
already a documented stability outlier), while the API models show the
opposite asymmetry. **This is no longer just an n_models=5 lead** — at the
current n_models=8 tier-matched comparison, `analysis/COMPARISON_A_INTERPRETATION.md`
independently confirms the same asymmetry with a proper paired-Wilcoxon
rank-biserial effect size (6 of 8 models favor Dynamic-L4; only the two
Qwen models favor ULT, by a large enough margin to dominate the raw column
mean without dominating the rank-based vote) — still not proof of a
contamination mechanism specifically, but corroborated by a second,
independent statistical reading rather than resting on this section's
n=5 observation alone. It is the kind of pattern a contamination story
would produce (open-weight models plausibly saw ULT-adjacent web content a
leakage-free Dynamic instantiation can't replicate) — recorded as a lead
for RQ1 cross-referencing, not a finding established here.

## Write-up stub — Discussion chapter (expand from here, not a final draft)

This project's construct validity for the Dynamic benchmark rests on two
complementary arguments. The **top-down** argument (§2 corpus design, §5
tier/mutation-operator specification) is definitional: the benchmark is
built directly from an explicit, pre-specified model of what test-generation
capability consists of (complexity-tiered problem structure, a fixed
mutation-operator catalogue, docstring-synonym variation) — validity is
designed in, not discovered after the fact. The **bottom-up** argument (this
RQ5/H6 analysis) is empirical: does the benchmark's ranking of models agree
with independent measures of related capability? The answer here is mixed
and instructive rather than simply confirmatory. **Updated 2026-08-12**:
cross-provider rank agreement with ULT (n_models=9, spanning distinct
architectures and providers) has moved off near-zero but still falls short
of the pre-registered validity-supporting band on the pre-registered
primary metric (Kendall τ=0.143→0.333→**0.278**, the last move
2026-08-11/12 as Gemini Pro Preview's Dynamic-L4 crawl completed), and
that finding is reported as-is, not smoothed over. A post-hoc diagnosis, however, complicates a simple
"distinct capabilities" reading — with a sharper edge than it first
appeared: most of the gap is attributable to how Mut@K conditions on
baseline-pass rather than to Dynamic and ULT disagreeing about model
capability outright, and a coverage-adjusted re-scoring that folds the
baseline-pass rate back in recovers τ=0.944 (up from 0.905). But
decomposing that recovered agreement shows it is almost entirely a
*baseline-pass-rate* story, not a mutation-quality one: baseline_pass_rate
alone already correlates at τ=0.889 (up from 0.810) across benchmarks,
while Mut@K conditional on passing (the actual pre-registered metric)
stays substantially lower at τ=0.333 even within that same matched
comparison. Disattenuating for Dynamic's own measurement reliability moves
the raw number only modestly (see `analysis/RQ5_DIAGNOSTICS.md` sec 4 for
the current, live-computed figure), while the coverage-adjusted score's own
(much higher, and independently verified) reliability leaves ITS
disattenuated figure essentially unchanged — reliability is not the
limiting factor for either reading. **A further check against the
pre-registered interpretation frame itself (`analysis/RQ5_ANALYSIS_PLAN.md`,
Spearman ρ 0.4–0.8 = validity-supporting, near-perfect = redundant)
sharpens this further: τ=0.944 sits at or above the "redundant" line, not
inside the validity-supporting zone.** So the
honest statement is two-sided, more specific, and less comfortable than a
first pass suggests: the metric as pre-registered and specified does rank
models inconsistently across benchmarks on mutation-quality-conditional-
on-passing (the validity-undermining zone), while the coverage-adjusted
score's cross-benchmark agreement is strong enough to raise a redundancy
concern rather than resolve a validity one. Neither candidate reading of
this benchmark's primary metric lands in the zone the plan predicted in
advance. The one
clean, strong correlation available (Dynamic/ULT vs BigCodeBench, n_models=3) is
confined to a single model family's size sweep and is better read as
evidence that scale predicts everything within a family than as independent
proxy validation across the field. Taken together, the two halves of
construct validity point in a genuinely open direction rather than a
foregone conclusion: the top-down design is principled, and the bottom-up
diagnosis suggests the underlying capabilities are more related than the
headline cross-provider number implies once measurement conditioning is
accounted for — but that accounting is itself a post-hoc correction, not a
pre-registered result, and a genuinely cross-provider public-benchmark
comparison (contingent on models this recent — GPT-5, Claude Sonnet
4.6/Haiku 4.5, Gemini 3.1 — ever appearing on a BigCodeBench-style
leaderboard, which may simply never happen for snapshots this new) remains
the evidence that would actually settle which reading is closer to correct.

## Parts 2-3 result: Dynamic/ULT vs BigCodeBench, N=3, all-Qwen

The leaderboard harvest (`analysis/RQ5_LEADERBOARD_HARVEST.md`) found
published, snapshot-matched BigCodeBench numbers for all 3 Qwen sizes and
none of the six API models — so no self-run was needed, but the
public-benchmark correlation is **N=3, and all three points are the same
model family at three sizes** (Qwen 1.5B/7B/32B):

| Pair | n | Pearson r | Spearman ρ | Kendall τ | permutation p (τ) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dynamic(pooled) ↔ BigCodeBench (Complete) | 3 | 0.981 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.331 |
| ULT ↔ BigCodeBench (Complete) | 3 | 0.927 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.331 |
| Dynamic(pooled) ↔ BigCodeBench (Hard) | 3 | 0.992 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.331 |
| ULT ↔ BigCodeBench (Hard) | 3 | 0.949 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.331 |

**Read this as size-scaling, not cross-benchmark validity, and say so
explicitly.** A perfect rank match across a 1.5B→7B→32B sweep within one
model family is close to the least surprising result available: bigger
Qwen-Coder checkpoints are better at essentially everything, so of course
they rank the same way on Dynamic, ULT, and BigCodeBench. This is NOT the
same kind of evidence as the n_models=9 Dynamic↔ULT result above (updated
2026-08-07, was n_models=7), which spans multiple providers/architectures
and is the test that can actually fail (and did show weak, though no
longer near-zero, rank agreement). The permutation p-value confirms
this isn't statistically distinguishable from chance either (p=0.331 even
at "perfect" τ=1.0 — n_models=3 has only 6 possible orderings, so a perfect match
isn't rare enough to be significant). **Do not report the n_models=3 numbers as
"BigCodeBench validates this benchmark"** — report them as "consistent
with, but not independent evidence for, cross-benchmark validity," and
lead with the cross-provider n_models=9 Dynamic↔ULT result as the analysis that
actually stress-tests the claim.

| ULT↔public | coverage-adjusted | 3→7 | 1.000 → **0.714** (CI [0.06, 1.00], p=0.032 — holds, weaker) |

**This is exactly the confirmation the "read this as size-scaling, not
cross-benchmark validity" caution above was waiting on.** The raw
pre-registered metric's perfect n_models=3 correlation was indeed a within-family
size-scaling artifact — it falls apart the moment real architectural
diversity enters the comparison (τ 1.000→0.429/0.238, both statistically
indistinguishable from chance). The coverage-adjusted score's correlation
survives this test essentially intact (τ 1.000→0.905 for Dynamic, a real,
significant, n_models=7, 4-family result) — independent, external evidence for
the same "baseline-pass-rate agreement is real and robust" finding as the
Dynamic↔ULT internal comparison above, now validated against a public
benchmark this project had no hand in constructing.

Spearman ρ between scores," no Fisher z required) **is answerable
entirely from the table above and its Pearson/Spearman companions,
already computed for clause 1.** Reported here as a hypothesis result,
not a gap — see `analysis/HYPOTHESIS_DEVIATIONS.md` for the full ledger
entry this section summarizes.

**Dynamic is the stronger (or tied) correlation in all 12 comparisons —
never weaker.** At cross-family n=7 (the scope that isn't ceiling-limited
by n=3's near-total lack of statistical resolution), Dynamic is strictly
stronger on every one of the 6 statistic-metric combinations, not just on
average. **Verdict: H6 clause 2 is NOT SUPPORTED** — direction is
opposite to the prediction. This is worth reading as a favourable finding
rather than a failed prediction: the dynamic benchmark tracks external
code-generation capability at least as well as, and by several measures
better than, the static benchmark it was designed as a
contamination-resistant alternative to.

**Caveats that travel with this table**, the same ones already disclosed
for clause 1's cross-family numbers below but not previously attached at
this specific comparison: 2 of the 7 cross-family points are individually
thin-cell (CodeLlama-13B: ULT n_defined=8; Mistral-7B: Dynamic
n_defined=5, ULT n_defined=1 — both below this project's own
n_defined<20 thinness threshold, part of why several of the CIs above are
wide); 2 of the 7 cross-family models have chat-template dates postdating
their own published BigCodeBench eval date; StarCoder2 required a
non-identical prompt-delivery accommodation (full detail: "Cross-family
extension — limitations" below).

**Bottom-line construct-validity statement, checked directly against the
pre-registered frame (`analysis/RQ5_ANALYSIS_PLAN.md`), not smoothed over.**
That frame set three zones in advance: near-zero = undermines validity,
Spearman ρ roughly 0.4–0.8 = validity-supporting, near-perfect = makes the
benchmark **redundant** ("if Dynamic's ranking were fully predictable from a
generic code-gen leaderboard, there would be no reason to run a separate
test-generation benchmark at all"). Neither of this analysis's two headline
numbers lands in the validity-supporting zone:

- The pre-registered primary (conditional Mut@K, τ=0.278 as of
  2026-08-12 — moved from 0.143 [2026-07-xx] to 0.333 [2026-08-07] to
  0.278 [2026-08-11/12, Gemini Pro Preview's Dynamic-L4 crawl completing]
  — and its cross-family echo τ=0.359, similarly down from 0.385) still
  falls short of the 0.4-0.8 validity-supporting band — closer to it than
  the original n=7 reading, but reported as still short, not re-litigated
  as a win.
- The coverage-adjusted score (τ=0.944 canonical, updated from 0.905 —
  and itself τ=0.889 if mechanically-repaired rows are excluded, per the
  sanitizer-sensitivity caveat above; 0.897 cross-family internal, updated
  from 0.855; 0.905/0.714 cross-family external, unaffected by this
  landing) sits at or above the near-perfect end — by the pre-registered
  frame's own logic, **this is the "redundant" zone, not the
  "validity-supporting" one**, under either the 0.944 or the 0.889
  reading. Presenting 0.944 as an unqualified win misreads the frame that was written down
  specifically to prevent exactly that reading. Taken at face value, a
  benchmark whose coverage-adjusted score predicts a generic code-gen
  leaderboard this well is not adding much test-generation-specific
  signal beyond "can this model produce working code at all" — which is
  close to what BigCodeBench's own Pass@1 already measures.

**So the honest two-sided statement is not "the benchmark validates via the
coverage-adjusted score" — it is that NEITHER of this project's two
candidate primary-metric readings lands where the pre-registered frame
called validity-supporting.** The conditional metric is too disconnected
from external benchmarks; the coverage-adjusted metric is disconnected in
the other direction, tracking external benchmarks closely enough to raise
a redundancy concern rather than resolve it. What's left, and what actually
IS informative, is the diagnosis itself (secs above, `RQ5_DIAGNOSTICS.md`):
this benchmark's headline metric decomposes cleanly into a baseline-pass-
rate component that is highly redundant with generic coding ability
(consistent with "redundant") and a mutation-quality-conditional-on-passing
component whose cross-benchmark signal, even after the 2026-08-07 landing
moved it off near-zero (τ=0.333), is still not statistically distinguishable
from no relationship (raw p=0.254) and still sits below the pre-registered
validity-supporting band (consistent with "undermines validity", though sec 2b's noise-floor audit
shows part of that near-zero reading is a resolving-power artifact among
closely-scored models, not necessarily genuine disagreement for every
pair). Neither component, nor their product, is the "related but distinct,
ρ 0.4–0.8" result the plan predicted in advance. This should be written up
as a genuine, unresolved tension for the construct-validity argument, not
narrated past.

## Cross-family extension (approved 2026-07-28) — limitations

Full-scale Dynamic (L1-L4, inst1) + ULT core_400 runs for 4 non-Qwen
candidates (DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B-Instruct, CodeLlama-13B-Instruct,
StarCoder2-15B-Instruct-v0.1, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3) are the mechanism
for breaking the Parts 2-3 all-Qwen size-sweep confound above. Two
limitations to disclose alongside whatever correlation these runs produce,
not discovered after the fact:

**Chat-template drift relative to each model's published BigCodeBench
number.** Snapshot verification (`analysis/RQ5_CROSS_FAMILY_PROPOSAL.md`)
confirmed no weight-file-changing commit since each model's eval date for
all 4 candidates — but weights are not the whole story for a chat model;
the *template* that turns messages into a prompt string matters too, and
two of the four postdate their own eval:
- **CodeLlama-13B-Instruct**: `tokenizer_config.json`'s `chat_template` was
  added 2024-03-04 — its BigCodeBench eval date is 2023-08-25. At eval
  time this model most likely had NO chat template at all; BigCodeBench's
  own harness would have applied its own Llama-style formatting. This
  project's harness applies the template that exists TODAY.
- **Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3**: tokenizer/chat-template adjustments landed
  Aug 2024 — its eval date is 2024-05-22.
- DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B-Instruct and StarCoder2-15B-Instruct-v0.1's templates
  predate or coincide with their eval dates — no equivalent concern.

Practically: our own Dynamic/ULT numbers for these two models are internally
valid (every model in this project is prompted via the same
`apply_chat_template()` call, consistently) but are **not guaranteed to
reproduce the exact prompt formatting that produced their published
BigCodeBench Pass@1** — a cross-family correlation against those published
numbers should note this rather than treat all 4 candidates' published
figures as equally trustworthy anchors. This is a prompt-FORMATTING
provenance gap, not a weight-snapshot one; the two are logged and verified
independently (`chat_template_hash`/`chat_template_source` in
`src/models/local.py` output vs. the HF commit-history check).

**StarCoder2-15B-Instruct-v0.1's prompt delivery differs from the other
three.** Its `chat_template` unconditionally rejects a `role="system"`
message (verified by reading the template source directly, not inferred).
Accommodated via `supports_system_role: false`
(`configs/dry_run_ult_starcoder2_15b.yaml` and its full-scale configs): the
system instruction is folded into the first user turn instead of sent
separately (`src/data/test_gen.py::_build_messages`), logged per-row as
`prompt_delivery_mode` (`"system_role"` for the other 3 candidates and every
canonical model, `"system_folded_into_user"` for StarCoder2). The
instruction TEXT is identical either way — only its delivery differs — but
this means StarCoder2's results are not from a byte-identical prompt
structure to the other candidates, a genuine (small, disclosed) methodological
asymmetry within the cross-family cohort itself, separate from the
published-benchmark comparison above.

**Thin-cell inputs to the cross-family n=7 correlation itself (added
2026-08-12 — this is the caveat missing from this section before this
pass, per `analysis/WORKBOOK_VERIFICATION.md`).** 2 of the 7 cross-family
data points feeding every n=7 correlation cited in this document
(Dynamic↔ULT, Dynamic↔BigCodeBench, ULT↔BigCodeBench, both raw and
coverage-adjusted) are individually thin-cell by this project's own
n_defined<20 threshold: **CodeLlama-13B** (ULT n_defined=8) and
**Mistral-7B** (Dynamic n_defined=5, ULT n_defined=1 — see the floor
verification immediately below for why Mistral's own count is this low).
Their own per-model means are shakier point estimates than the other 5
candidates', which widens several of this document's n=7 correlation
CIs without necessarily changing the point estimate — the caveat should
travel with any citation of an n=7 cross-family figure, not just the ones
in this section.

**Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 floor verification (2026-07-28).** Its full-scale
Dynamic run landed a baseline_pass_rate of just 1.7% (5/300) — by far the
lowest of the four cross-family candidates and comparable to Qwen 1.5B's
established floor behavior. Before treating this as a real capability
finding rather than a harness artifact (chat-template drift was already
flagged as a live concern for this model), inspected 5 raw completions
end-to-end, one per tier plus a duplicate at L4, all with `exec_rate=1.0`
(collectible) and `baseline_passed=False`, plus 2 of the 10 `exec_rate=0.0`
rows:
- **`extracted_test_file` matches the raw `response` verbatim in all 7
  cases** (modulo the markdown code-fence stripping) — extraction did its
  job; nothing here is a parsing artifact.
- **L1-coerce-002, L2-validator-001, L3-search-001, L4-aggregate-001,
  L4-pipeline-001** (all `exec=1.0`, `baseline_passed=False`): syntactically
  clean, well-formed test files that assert **specific, concrete, wrong**
  numeric values for what the function actually returns (e.g.
  `test_encode_input_normal_case` asserts `encode_input(5, 2) == [5, 15]`
  against the real implementation's actual output) — the model can write a
  valid, importable test but reasons incorrectly about the function's real
  behavior. Notably this shows up even at **L1**, this project's easiest
  tier, where every other model tested so far (canonical and cross-family)
  sits at or near ceiling — this is a genuine, tier-independent capability
  gap for this model, not a hard-tier-only effect.
- **L1-format-001** (`exec=0.0`, "collection error"): the model's own
  generation degenerates into an endless repeated-digit string
  (`...9999999999...`, response length 2265 chars, cut off mid-digit-string
  by `max_new_tokens`) — the same repetition-collapse failure mode already
  documented for Qwen 1.5B's L4 seeds 1101/1102
  (`analysis/STABILITY_INTERPRETATION.md`), just triggered here at L1 rather
  than only at the hardest tier.
- **L1-arithmetic-001** (`exec=0.0`, "collection error"): a complete,
  ordinary-looking test file with no chat-template pollution or stray
  tokens — the collection failure itself traces to the model's own
  content (mismatched function signature/name assumption for this
  instance), not to prompt construction.

None of the 7 inspected rows show any sign of the chat-template-drift caveat
above actually corrupting output (no stray special tokens, no malformed
structure, no truncated/duplicated framing) — the low pass rate is a
genuine capability-limitation finding for this model on this benchmark, by
the same evidence standard already applied to Qwen 1.5B, not a
template-drift or extraction artifact.

## Cross-definition reconciliation (added 2026-08-03, cited figures refreshed 2026-08-07)

Every "coverage-adjusted" number in this document (τ=0.944 canonical
[updated 2026-08-07 from 0.905], 0.897 cross-family [from 0.855], 0.905/0.714
vs BigCodeBench [unaffected by the 2026-08-07 landing]) is **numerically
unchanged by this reconciliation note itself** — the note is about which
methodology a metric matches, not a data update, and data landing since
2026-08-03 is a separate, later event from what this section documents.
What this note changes is the justification: a 2026-07-30 note
(`src/eval/test_gen_scorer.py`, `analysis/RQ5_CROSS_DEFINITION_AUDIT.md`)
had concluded, from an LLM-summarized reading of the ULT/PLT paper, that
coverage-adjusted Mut@K "matches the ULT paper's own methodology."
Re-verified 2026-08-03 against raw (non-summarized) paper text AND the
paper's own published code (`github.com/huangd1999/UnLeakedTestBench`):
the paper's PROSE does read that way, but its own CODE
(`Ray/main.py::mutation_statistic()`, the function its CLI actually
invokes) computes a baseline-pass-CONDITIONED mean — the same estimand as
this project's pre-registered primary (conditional Mut@K, τ=0.333/0.385,
updated 2026-08-07 from 0.143/0.345), not the zero-filled one its prose
describes. Full evidence and the algebraic proof that coverage-adjusted
exactly equals the zero-filled/prose-matched reading:
`analysis/RQ5_CROSS_DEFINITION_AUDIT.md`.

**This sharpens rather than undermines the "redundant zone" reading
above**: the metric that happens to match ULT's own achieved
implementation (conditional Mut@K) is the one with the weakest cross-
benchmark agreement; the metric with the strong, "redundant"-zone
agreement (coverage-adjusted) matches only the paper's stated formula, not
its code. Neither fact changes which zone τ=0.944 or τ=0.333 fall into —
both were already read against the pre-registered plan's zones above,
unaffected by this reconciliation — but "coverage-adjusted is the
literature-matching score, so redundancy is the literature's problem too"
would be an overreach this note forecloses: coverage-adjusted matches the
literature's STATED method, not its ACHIEVED one.

## Holm-Bonferroni correction (added 2026-08-04, p-values updated 2026-08-07)

PROJECT_BRIEF.md §3.1 requires Holm-Bonferroni correction "within each RQ
family" — never applied anywhere in this project until now
(`analysis/HYPOTHESIS_COVERAGE_AUDIT.md`). Applied to the 12 headline
Kendall-tau tests this document narrates (3 benchmark pairs × 2 scopes ×
2 metric versions — Hard-split pairs excluded as a secondary check, not
part of this headline family; `scripts/analyze_rq5_cross_family.py`,
`analysis/RQ5_CROSS_FAMILY_PROVENANCE.md` has the full table): **4/12
significant at raw p<0.05, 3/12 after Holm correction — same counts as
before the 2026-08-07 data landing**, though the individual Holm-adjusted
p-values shifted (Holm's stepwise procedure depends on the rank-ordering
of all 12 p-values, so even the 8 BigCodeBench-side rows whose own raw p
didn't move can still get a different adjusted p when the other 4 rows'
raw p-values change). The one that drops out is still the weakest of the
four — ULT↔public, cross-family, coverage-adjusted (τ=0.714, raw p=0.032,
Holm-adjusted p=0.285, unchanged since none of the newly-landed models have
published BigCodeBench scores). **The three results this write-up leans on
hardest survive, with materially tighter Holm-adjusted p-values now**:
Dynamic↔ULT coverage-adjusted (canonical scope: Holm-adjusted p=0.037→**0.0022**;
cross-family scope: Holm-adjusted p=0.001→**0.0012**, essentially unchanged)
and Dynamic↔public coverage-adjusted cross-family (Holm-adjusted
p=0.037→**0.034**). Every raw-Mut@K correlation was already
non-significant uncorrected and stays that way. **Practical effect on
this document's conclusions: none of the qualitative claims above change**
— the coverage-adjusted score's cross-benchmark agreement was never
claimed to hold for the ULT↔public pair specifically as strongly as for
the other two pairs (the "Bottom-line construct-validity statement"
already treats τ=0.944/0.897 as the headline evidence, with the
BigCodeBench comparisons as corroborating, not load-bearing, evidence) —
but the ULT↔public cross-family number should not be quoted as
independently significant going forward without this caveat.
