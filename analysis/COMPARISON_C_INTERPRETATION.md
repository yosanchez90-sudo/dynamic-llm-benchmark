# Comparison C — headline interpretation

Hand-written, not auto-generated. `analysis/COMPARISON_C_PROVENANCE.md`
(regenerated by `scripts/analyze_comparison_c.py`) holds the raw tables this
interpretation refers to — re-run that script freely after new data lands;
it will not touch this file.

**The predicted pattern holds at the top and bottom of the gradient, with one
real deviation in the middle, now explained.** seen-ULT gains +99.1pp (CI
[97.3, 100.0]) — essentially every seen problem now passes baseline,
matching the 112/112 (100.0%) verbatim-match finding
(`data/raw/ult_plt/PROVENANCE.md`). At the other end, Dynamic-L4 shows
exactly 0.0pp gain, but this is **floor-limited, not evidence of "no
transfer"**: both PRE and POST have baseline_pass_rate identically 0.000 (a
1.5B model can't clear the CC>=8 baseline gate at all, before or after
fine-tuning) — the ANALYSIS_PLAN.md amendment's whole reason for adding the
L1-L3 arm.

## Deviation from the strict monotone-decay prediction — explained, not just reported

PLT's raw gain (+22.5pp) is *larger* than unseen-ULT's (+15.6pp), not
smaller — gain(seen-ULT) > **gain(PLT) > gain(unseen-ULT)** > gain(Dynamic),
not the predicted gain(seen-ULT) > gain(unseen-ULT) > gain(PLT) >
gain(Dynamic).

**Selection-effect check** (`scripts/audit_unseen_ult_vs_plt.py`,
`analysis/comparison_c_unseen_vs_plt_audit.csv`, 2026-07-27): unseen-ULT
(n=288) and PLT core_400 (n=400) are not comparable populations. unseen-ULT
is *defined* by NOT having a verified training pair — every one of its 288
problems falls into a named defect category from the repair taxonomy, with
no "randomly not selected" remainder: STRUCTURAL 34% (code can't execute at
all, e.g. a missing import), SHAPE_NOT_REPAIRABLE 37% (runs, but the
assertion shape is too complex for the simple `assert x == y` auto-repair),
and a previously unlabelled 28% where the shipped ground-truth `tests`
field itself is not valid Python (confirmed directly via `ast.parse` on
samples — unclosed brackets, garbled/truncated text — silently dropped by
`repair_ult_ground_truth.py`'s `SyntaxError` catch rather than classified).
PLT core_400 has no analogous screening at all: its `test_input` field
never contained real assertions to classify in the first place
(`data/raw/ult_plt/PROVENANCE.md`'s original "Known discrepancy" note), so
it is a plain CC-stratified sample, unfiltered by any test-quality
criterion.

The numbers back this up: unseen-ULT's code is significantly longer than
PLT's (mean 1533 vs 1220 chars, Mann-Whitney p<0.0001) and its PRE
baseline_pass_rate is barely a third of PLT's (2.4% vs 7.5%) — CC itself is
matched (14.97 vs 15.45, p=0.87), so complexity is not the driver. With 34%
of unseen-ULT being code that literally cannot execute regardless of what
test is written, a chunk of that population has a hard ceiling of zero no
matter how good the model gets — an artificial floor baked into the
population, not a content-specific "ULT doesn't transfer" story. **This is
the explanation for the inversion**: PLT looks easier to improve on because
it is, structurally, an easier (unscreened) population — not because
leaked-twin content is more exploitable than genuine ULT content.

## The Dynamic-L1-L3 robustness control is the more informative read

It has real (non-floor) PRE headroom (6.7%) and shows its own +8.7pp gain
(CI [5.6, 12.0]) — fine-tuning on ULT improves test-writing on completely
unrelated, never-seen Dynamic problems too. That's a genuine "fine-tuning
helps in general" effect, and subtracting it as a control changes the
picture:
- seen-ULT: 99.1pp -> **90.4pp** DiD (CI [86.5, 94.1]) — still overwhelming.
- PLT: 22.5pp -> **13.8pp** DiD (CI [7.7, 20.1]) — clearly real, CI well clear of zero.
- unseen-ULT: 15.6pp -> **6.9pp** DiD (CI [1.3, 13.0]) — barely excludes
  zero. Most of unseen-ULT's raw gain is the generic-improvement effect,
  not something ULT-content-specific; what's left is a small, borderline
  signal, not the confidently-real transfer the raw number suggested — and
  per the selection-effect check above, even that residual is measured on
  a population a third of which has a hard structural ceiling.

## Mut@K tells a different, complementary story about test *quality*

Even though seen-ULT reaches 100% baseline-pass and 100% verbatim
reproduction, its POST Mut@K is only **0.438** (n=112, fully defined now
that every suite passes) — memorizing ULT's own ground-truth tests verbatim
does not make them *good* tests. This is a ceiling inherited from ULT's own
repair-verified ground truth (`data/raw/ult_plt/PROVENANCE.md`'s
ground-truth-quality section), not a fine-tuning artifact: perfect
reproduction of a mediocre test suite is still a mediocre test suite. Every
other arm's POST Mut@K is measured on a small, PRE-side-limited n (see the
analysis plan's "PRE data sparsity" section) and should not be
over-interpreted — e.g. unseen-ULT's mean *drops* from 0.338 (n=7, PRE) to
0.139 (n=52, POST) simply because the 45 newly-unlocked suites are, on
average, weaker than the tiny pre-existing PRE set, not because fine-tuning
made tests worse.

## Onset curve — two timescales, not one

`analysis/figures/comparison_c_onset_curve.png` (epochs {0,1,3,5,10,20,30},
fixed 50-seen/50-unseen/75-Dynamic-L1L2 subsample) shows two genuinely
different processes on two different timescales, not a single smooth trend:

1. **A fast, one-time generic-improvement jump, epoch 0 -> 1, all three
   arms.** Every arm (seen-ULT, unseen-ULT, Dynamic-L1/L2) jumps from ~0-2%
   PRE to roughly 10-26% by epoch 1 — this is the same generic
   "fine-tuning helps" effect the Dynamic-L1-L3 robustness control
   isolates above, arriving almost immediately and hitting the untrained
   arms just as much as the trained one.
2. **A slower, memorization-specific climb, epochs 5-20, seen-ULT only.**
   After that shared first-epoch jump, unseen-ULT and Dynamic-L1/L2 go
   flat and stay noisy in the 16-23% band all the way to epoch 30 — no
   further trend. seen-ULT instead keeps climbing on its own: 20% (epoch
   3, noise) -> 34% (epoch 5) -> 72% (epoch 10) -> 100% (epoch 20, holds
   through 30). Verbatim-match tracks this second process almost exactly
   and lags slightly behind it: 2% (epoch 1) -> 2% (epoch 3) -> 14% (epoch
   5) -> 62% (epoch 10) -> 100% (epoch 20) — consistent with the
   1,014-example training set's ~127 gradient-steps/epoch (vs ~2 for the
   16-example supplementary run), i.e. onset arriving far earlier in
   training than the supplementary study's curves, as anticipated when the
   dense-early/sparse-late epoch grid was chosen.

Reading the whole curve as one trend would conflate these two effects; the
figure is deliberately captioned to keep them apart.
