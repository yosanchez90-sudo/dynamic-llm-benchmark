to-high rank agreement, most pairs significant) is unchanged either way.

### Why score-level stability (0.82-0.97) reads higher than rank-level tau (0.68)

The gap is a genuine rank-vs-score effect, not a data problem — earlier interim reads of tau (0.84 on
~61/180 cells, then 0.593 on ~104/180 cells — see `PROJECT_BRIEF.md` §11) swung much
more than the StableMut@K point estimates did, because **many models cluster closely
in absolute score** (0.82-0.97 for six of the ten model variants). Tau is a rank
statistic — it's sensitive to small, noisy score differences flipping the relative
order of two closely-scored models, even when neither model's own score moved much.
This is why the by-model StableMut@K table is the more robust stability claim, and the
Kendall tau table should be reported with its per-pair n and p-values visible (as
above), not as a single blended number — a reader should be able to see that the
weaker pairs are exactly the ones with the fewest models (n=7) and are not individually
significant, rather than have that folded into one mean.
